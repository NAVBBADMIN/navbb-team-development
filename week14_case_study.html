Case Study · Week 14

Same Sign-Up. Different Show Rate.

~8 min · Click through each scene · One question at the end.
Scene 1 of 6
The Setup

Same number of sign-ups. Same territory size. One recruiter has a 40% show rate. The other has 80%.

Recruiter A and Recruiter B both had a solid month of sign-ups. Same total count, similar daycare mix, similar types of dogs. But when the collection team looks at first-screening show rates, the gap is stark.

The difference isn't in how they recruit. It's in what happens in the 60 seconds after someone says yes.

Recruiter A — The Handoff

Recruiter A gets the yes — and then says "great, we'll be in touch."

After a parent fills out the registration form, Recruiter A says "awesome, someone will reach out to schedule your first screening." She doesn't give them a specific date. She doesn't ask them to put anything in their calendar. She takes the form and moves on to the next parent.

She follows up three weeks later with a group email to all pending donors. The subject line says "Next Steps." Seven people registered. Two open the email. One shows up to screening.

Recruiter B — The 60 Seconds

Recruiter B gets the yes — and doesn't stop there.

After a parent fills out the registration form, Recruiter B says "amazing — okay, we're going to be back here in two weeks, that'll be a Tuesday the 7th. That's your first screening. Can you put that in your phone right now while I have you?" Most parents pull out their phones and do it.

She writes down the dog's name, the parent's name, and one thing from the conversation. Two days before the screening, she sends a specific message: "Hi Sarah — just wanted to remind you that Cooper's screening is this Tuesday the 7th. He's going to do great."

The Difference in the Room

Same location. Same screening day. Very different outcomes at the door.

On screening day, the collection team knows exactly what to expect from Recruiter B's pending list. Most of them have confirmed. A few have responded to the reminder with "we'll be there!" One said they couldn't make it and asked about next time — which is a useful signal, not a no.

From Recruiter A's list, two people show up. Three didn't respond to the group email. Two said they forgot. The dogs who didn't come still need blood to be available for them if they ever need a transfusion. The urgency was real in the lobby. It was gone by screening day.

One Month Later

Same sign-up count. Very different active donor numbers.

Recruiter A — 40% show rate

7 sign-ups this month. 3 made it to first screening. 2 passed and are active donors. 5 are in a "pending" limbo with no specific next step. Recruiter A will follow up with another group email. Some of those 5 will eventually come in. Some won't. Recruiter A doesn't know which.

Recruiter B — 80% show rate

7 sign-ups this month. 6 made it to first screening. 5 passed and are active donors. 1 couldn't make it and rescheduled. Recruiter B knows each of them by name, knows which dog belongs to which parent, and already knows who she's following up with and why. Her pipeline is clean.

The difference between 3 active donors and 5 active donors doesn't sound huge. Across a year, across a full roster of locations, it determines whether the program has enough blood.

Your Turn

Think about your last three sign-ups. Did you give them a specific date? Did you help them put it in their calendar? Do you know if they're coming?

Be honest. Not about what you plan to do — about what you actually did in those moments. If the answer is "I said we'd be in touch," what would you do differently with the next sign-up?
