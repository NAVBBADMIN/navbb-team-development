The Walking-Away Owner

~8 min · Click through each scene · One question at the end.
Scene 1 of 6
The Setup

An older gentleman. A beautiful golden retriever. The standard pitch has not landed.

He walked into the daycare lobby to pick up his dog. He listened politely while the recruiter explained the program. He nodded a few times. He did not ask any questions. His body language made it clear: he was about to say "sounds interesting" and walk out the door.

This happens every day. A parent hears the pitch, it does not land, and they leave. Sometimes they take a flyer. Sometimes they do not. Almost always, that is the last interaction. Two recruiters, same lobby, same owner. Watch what happens next.

Recruiter A: Following the Script

Recruiter A adds more information. The owner keeps walking.

Recruiter A sees the owner starting to disengage. She adds more information: the program details, the frequency of donations, the health benefits for the dog. She hands him a flyer. She says the website has everything he needs to know.

He takes the flyer. He thanks her. He leaves. Recruiter A does not have his name. She does not have any way to follow up. She has given him everything there is to give and he is gone.

The information was not the problem. More information was not the solution. He already had enough information to decide. He just had not been given a reason to stay in the conversation.

Recruiter B: The Pivot

Recruiter B stops talking about the program and starts talking about the dog.

Recruiter B also sees the owner starting to disengage. Instead of adding more information, she changes the frame. She stops talking about what the program needs and starts talking about what the program does for the dog.

"The thing I want you to understand is that we are actually making a real investment in your dog's health. Before he ever donates, we do full bloodwork, we put him on parasite prevention, we monitor him on an ongoing basis. We want to make the greatest impact with that investment — and we can only do that with dogs who come consistently."

He stopped walking. He turned around. "What kind of bloodwork?" he asked. That was the beginning of a real conversation.

The Conversation That Followed

The investment frame opened a door that the standard pitch had closed.

He was 68. The golden retriever was 4, named Arthur. He had never thought about his dog as a blood donor, but he thought a lot about Arthur's long-term health. The idea that the program was monitoring Arthur, tracking his bloodwork, keeping him on parasite prevention — that mattered to him.

"So you are saying this is actually good for him," he said.

"Yes," Recruiter B said. "The health monitoring alone is something most owners never get because they only see the vet when something is wrong. Our dogs come in regularly. We catch things early. It is genuinely good for the dog."

He asked for information. He gave her his name. He said he needed to talk to his wife but he was genuinely interested. Recruiter B has his name and a reason to follow up.

What Made the Difference

He did not need more information about the program. He needed a reason to care about it.

What did not work

More program details. More information about frequency, logistics, website resources. He already had enough to make a decision. The information was not moving him. Adding more of what was not working was not going to start working.

What worked

Shifting to what the program does for the dog. Making the investment concrete: bloodwork, parasite prevention, health monitoring. Framing the commitment not as something being asked of him, but as something he is getting access to in exchange for his dog showing up consistently.

The walking-away owner was not a lost cause. He was a parent who had not yet been given a frame that connected. The investment frame gave him one. He stopped. He turned around. He gave her his name. That is the whole job.

Your Turn

Think of an owner you lost recently — someone who seemed interested and then walked away. What frame were you using? Would the investment frame have changed anything?

Be specific. What did you say? What did they respond to? Where did the conversation stall? If you tried the investment frame, what do you think would have happened differently?
