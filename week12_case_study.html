Two Recruiters, Same Territory

~8 min · Click through each scene · One question at the end.
Scene 1 of 6
The Setup

Same territory size. Same donor count at the start. Very different approaches.

Recruiter A and Recruiter B inherit similar territories at the same time. Similar number of partner locations, similar number of active donors, similar mix of strong and developing relationships. Over the next three months, their results diverge significantly.

Recruiter A adds more locations. Recruiter B goes deeper into the ones she has. Watch what happens.

Recruiter A — Wider

Recruiter A has twelve locations on her schedule by month two.

When results are slow at a location, Recruiter A's instinct is to add another one. She now visits twelve locations per week. She is very busy. Her conversations at each location are shorter because she has more places to be. She knows every location by name and visit frequency. She cannot name a single parent at nine of the twelve who would recognize her name.

12 locations on schedule. Average visit time per location: 22 minutes. Parents who would recognize her name if the manager mentioned it: 4. Parents who have contacted her between visits: 0.
Recruiter B — Deeper

Recruiter B keeps eight locations and spends the difference on people.

Recruiter B dropped two locations that had given her the polite brush-off for months. She redirected that time to her anchor locations. She now knows the names of eleven parents across her eight locations. Three of them have her number saved. She has followed up with specific parents by name after visits — not mass emails, individual messages.

At her strongest location, the manager texts her when a new large dog joins the daycare because she asked the manager to. That took one conversation to set up. It has produced four leads this month that she didn't have to go find.

Month Two — The Call

Recruiter B gets a call she didn't make happen.

A parent at one of Recruiter B's locations calls her on a Wednesday morning. The parent has been in the program for three months. She ran into a neighbor at a dog park whose golden retriever is three years old, 65 pounds, and completely healthy. The neighbor had never heard of the program. The parent gave her Recruiter B's number.

Recruiter B signed up a donor she never visited, never pitched, and never emailed. The sign-up came entirely from a relationship she built by remembering a parent's name and following up twice after visits. That parent became an advocate because she felt known, not managed.

Recruiter A did not get a call that week. Recruiter A visited four locations.

Month Three — The Count

Same territory. Three months later.

Recruiter A — 12 locations

14 new sign-ups over three months. 2 parents who have her number saved. 0 inbound referrals. 3 locations paused or unresponsive that she's still visiting out of habit. Show rate on new donors: 58%.

Recruiter B — 8 locations

19 new sign-ups over three months, including 4 inbound referrals she didn't generate directly. 11 parents who know her name. 1 staff member who proactively flags new eligible dogs. Show rate on new donors: 79%.

Recruiter B did fewer visits and produced more donors. The difference was entirely in what she did with the time she had at each location.

Your Turn

At your anchor locations, how many parents know your name — and have you ever made a specific ask that turned a staff member into someone who works for you between visits?

Be specific. Not "I have good relationships there." A number. And if you've never asked a staff member to text you when a new eligible dog joins, what's stopped you from asking?
