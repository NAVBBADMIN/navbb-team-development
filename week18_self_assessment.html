A Patricia Moment
Patricia has eleven medically retired donors. Patricia has followed up with zero of their parents.

Patricia received the retirement notices. Patricia filed them. Patricia considers the relationship with the parent ended when the dog stopped being eligible. Patricia does not know the reason for most of the retirements. Patricia does not know whether any of them involve temporary conditions. Patricia scored herself a 3 on this self-assessment because she knows where the retired list is. Patricia is preparing a group email to all eleven parents reminding them of upcoming donation dates. Patricia has not noticed that none of these dogs are eligible to attend those dates.

Question 1 of 6 - Staying in Contact
When one of my donors gets medically retired, I continue to maintain contact with the parent at daycare visits rather than treating the relationship as ended.
Not outreach specifically about the retirement - just the ongoing relationship that makes a future reactivation conversation possible.
Question 2 of 6 - Staying in Contact
When a parent whose dog has been retired signals a change - mentions the medication stopped, brings up a vet visit, asks how to get back - I catch that signal and act on it.
The signal is not always direct. Sometimes it is a mention of a vet appointment. Sometimes it is the parent asking a question. Do you notice when it happens?
Question 3 of 6 - Knowing the Reason
For the medically retired dogs on my list, I know the reason for each retirement and whether that reason is likely temporary or permanent.
Medication is often temporary. Age thresholds are permanent. Bloodwork flags vary. Knowing the reason tells you whether to stay in the conversation.
Question 4 of 6 - Engaging the Medical Team
When I think a medically retired dog might be a candidate for reactivation, I know how to bring that question to the medical team - what to ask, what information to gather, and how to take the answer back to the parent.
The recruiter does not make the medical call. But the recruiter is the bridge between the parent who wants their dog to come back and the medical team who can say yes or no.
Question 5 of 6 - Stale Sign-Ups
When I encounter a parent who signed up a while ago and has not been active, I treat them as a re-engagement opportunity rather than writing them off as inactive.
Stale sign-ups are not the same as medical retirements but the outcome is similar: a parent who said yes and then went quiet. The fix is different but the attitude is the same.
Question 6 of 6 - The Retired List
I have reviewed my medically retired donor list recently and I know which dogs on it might have a realistic path back into the program.
Not as a project - as basic pipeline awareness. Knowing which retired dogs might return is part of managing the long-term health of your territory.
