Scene 1 of 6
One Week — Four Locations

Same recruiter. Different setup at every stop. Nothing cooperative.

It's a full week in the field. Every location on the schedule has something working against it — bad weather, a lobby the size of a hallway, an owner who won't return calls, and a location where she's never had a clear list of who's there before she shows up.

Each one of these is a real pattern. Each one of them has a real solution. Watch how she handles each one — and then watch what Patricia does at the same places.

Monday — Outdoor Drive-Thru Pickup

It's raining. The dogs come out a side door. The parents don't get out of their cars.

⚡ Outdoor / Drive-Thru

This is a location with solid potential — lots of large dogs, regular daycare attendance, parents who are generally friendly. But pickup is drive-thru. There's no lobby. In the rain, nobody's rolling down their windows to chat.

Patricia — Monday

Patricia stood in the parking lot in the rain. Patricia got wet. Patricia spoke to zero people. Patricia's notes say "low turnout due to weather." Patricia will try again next Tuesday. Same spot. Rain is expected.

Recruiter — Monday

She called ahead and asked the staff to flag any eligible dogs before pickup started. She positioned herself at the covered exit area — not in the parking lot — before the first car pulled up. Three conversations in twenty minutes. One sign-up. She left before it got worse.

The difference: She adapted her position and got the staff working with her before she arrived. Patricia reacted to the rain. She had already planned around it.

Tuesday — Lobby the Width of a Desk

Great location on paper. In person, there is nowhere to stand.

⚡ Tiny Lobby

This daycare has engaged owners and a good dog population. But the lobby is genuinely tiny — door, desk, door. No room to set up. No room to have a conversation without blocking the entire entrance. The first recruiter to visit didn't know this until she was already there.

Patricia — Tuesday

Patricia showed up and discovered there was no room. Patricia stood in the doorway. Two owners squeezed past her without making eye contact. Patricia left a stack of flyers on the front desk. The flyers are still there.

Recruiter — Tuesday

She'd heard the lobby was small, so she stopped by two days before the actual recruiting visit just to see it. She talked to the owner, walked the space, took a photo, and found a spot near the back door that worked. When she returned for the real visit, she knew exactly where to be. Two sign-ups.

The difference: She turned a surprise into a known quantity by making a low-stakes pre-visit. Patricia found out on the day of and had no plan B.

Wednesday — The Owner Who Isn't There

Great potential. Can't get five minutes with the decision-maker.

⚡ Missing Decision-Maker

This location has the ingredients — good dog count, friendly staff, owners who linger. But the manager who approves the partnership isn't available on any of the days she's visited. Staff is helpful but can't commit to anything. Emails go unanswered.

Patricia — Wednesday

Patricia emailed twice. Patricia called once. Patricia stopped by and spoke to the front desk. Patricia left her card. Patricia is waiting to hear back. Patricia has been waiting since March.

Recruiter — Wednesday

She stopped by unannounced on a different day of the week — not her recruiting day, just a pop-in. Brought pastries. Spent ten minutes with whoever was there, learned what the owner cared about, and got a real window for when she'd be in. She emailed the owner directly that evening with a personal note. The owner responded in four hours.

The difference: She changed her approach instead of repeating the same tactic. The unscheduled visit and the personal note did in one day what seven scheduled attempts hadn't done.

Thursday — No List, No Prep

Great location. No idea who's there until she walks in.

⚡ Information Gap

This is one of her better locations, but every visit starts from zero — she doesn't know which dogs are in that day until she gets there and asks. Staff is busy and doesn't always have time to pull a full list. She misses dogs that came and went before she arrived.

Patricia — Thursday

Patricia arrived at 4:30. Six dogs had already gone home. Patricia talked to the three owners who were still there. One was interested. Patricia did not get her name. Patricia will try again next week.

Recruiter — Thursday

She asked the daycare if she could get a look at their scheduling system — even just for an hour before pickup. They said yes. She built her list in advance, cross-referenced against dogs already in the program, and arrived knowing exactly who she wanted to talk to. Six sign-ups in a month at this location. Biscuit was on one of those lists.

The difference: She asked for something the location hadn't thought to offer. The staff was happy to help. She just had to ask directly and explain why.
