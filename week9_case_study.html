Case Study · Week 9

Same Competition, Two Operators

~8 min · Click through each scene · One question at the end.
Scene 1 of 6
Same Challenge. Same Scoring. Same Window.

Two recruiters. Identical competition design. Very different outcomes.

Recruiter A and Recruiter B are each running the partner location challenge. Same scoring — 2 points for each new approved donor, 1 point per donation-day appearance capped once per dog per month. Same challenge window. Two daycares each, selected for similar potential.

By the end of the challenge window, Recruiter B's locations have generated nearly three times the points of Recruiter A's. The competition design wasn't the difference. Watch what was.

The Launch — Recruiter A

Recruiter A launches well. Then stops.

Recruiter A pitches both locations on the same day. She's enthusiastic, explains the scoring, leaves a summary sheet at each location. Both managers seem engaged. One asks a question about whether boarding dogs count the same as daycare dogs. Recruiter A says she'll check and follow up.

0 follow-up messages sent about the boarding dog question. The manager kept thinking about it. The doubt quietly became a reason not to push parents as hard as she might have.

Recruiter A visits both locations on her normal schedule — roughly once a week. She doesn't mention the challenge score at either visit. She assumes the locations are tracking it themselves.

The Launch — Recruiter B

Recruiter B pitches them differently. On purpose.

Location C has a manager who is visibly competitive — she mentioned the daycare down the road twice in their first conversation. Recruiter B leads with FOMO: "The other location we're running this with has been in our program for a while. They're already ahead on donor numbers. This is your chance to change that." Manager is immediately locked in.

Location D is quieter, more relationship-driven. Recruiter B leads with community: "The locations that tend to do best in this are the ones where the staff genuinely knows their dogs and their parents. That's exactly what you have here."

Both managers can explain the scoring accurately before Recruiter B leaves. Every question gets answered in the room, not in a follow-up that never comes.

The Midpoint

This is where Recruiter A's challenge quietly dies.

Recruiter A visits both locations at the midpoint on her normal visit schedule. She doesn't bring up the score. She doesn't know it off the top of her head. She figures both locations are doing fine — neither has mentioned anything.

Recruiter A — Midpoint

Neither location knows their current score. Neither location has mentioned the challenge to a single parent this week. The challenge exists as a piece of paper somewhere. The manager at Location B still hasn't gotten an answer about boarding dogs.

Recruiter B — Midpoint

She visits Location C first and tells them they're ahead by four points. Manager pumps her fist. She visits Location D and tells them they're four points back — still winnable — and names exactly what would close the gap. Location D manager calls three parents that afternoon.

The Close

Both recruiters tally the scores. Only one of them built something lasting.

Recruiter A emails both locations the final results. Location C responds with a thumbs up. Location B doesn't respond. Neither location asks about running it again. Neither staff member mentions the program to parents more than they did before the challenge started.

Recruiter B visits both locations in person to close the challenge. Location C's manager asks when the next one is before Recruiter B can bring it up. Location D's manager says they'll get them next time — and means it. Both locations have added two to three new parents to their informal advocate list over the challenge window. Both staff contacts now mention the program to new daycare members without being asked.
