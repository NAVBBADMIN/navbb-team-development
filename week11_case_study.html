Two Recruiters, Same Tough Week

~8 min · Click through each scene · One question at the end.
Scene 1 of 6
The Setup

Same week. Same territory size. Same numbers so far. Very different responses.

Two recruiters hit a rough patch at the same time. Two maybes went cold. A partner location paused their participation. One visit produced nothing. It's Thursday and the week is not going to recover to where they wanted it.

Recruiter A and Recruiter B respond to the same week differently. Watch what changes — and what doesn't.

Recruiter A — The Grind Response

Recruiter A adds more. More visits. More follow-ups. More locations.

Recruiter A's instinct when things slow down is volume. If the numbers aren't moving, the answer is more activity. She adds two locations to next week's schedule. She sends follow-up emails to every cold lead in her list. She visits a location on Friday even though she's been three times this month and the manager has said he'll let her know.

She works harder than she normally would. She ends the week tired. The numbers didn't move. She starts the next week the same way, a little more discouraged, still with the same instinct: do more.

Recruiter B — The Step Back

Recruiter B stops. Not to quit. To remember.

Recruiter B has a ritual for slow weeks. She thinks about why she does this job. Not the program description — the actual reason. A dog that needed blood. A family that almost lost their dog. The donation that made the difference. She keeps a note she wrote to herself when she first started. She reads it on days like this.

When she goes back to the field on Thursday, something is different. Not her schedule. Not her territory. Her energy is different. The urgency in her pitch is real again because she just reconnected to what makes it urgent. She doesn't visit more locations. She has two better conversations than she's had all week.

The Conversation That Closes

Recruiter B's Thursday conversation ends in a sign-up. She knows why.

At her third location on Thursday, Recruiter B meets an owner she's spoken to twice before. Both times the owner was politely interested but noncommittal. This time Recruiter B opens differently — not with the program, but with what it prevents. She tells the story of a dog who needed a transfusion and a hospital that had to call around before they found blood.

The owner's dog had emergency surgery two years ago. She knows exactly what that search feels like from the other side. She signs up before Recruiter B finishes explaining the schedule.

The sign-up wasn't from a new location or a new tactic. It was from the same owner Recruiter B had already visited twice. What changed was the energy she brought into the conversation after reconnecting to why the work matters.

The Week's End

Same rough week. One recruiter ends it depleted. One ends it anchored.

Recruiter A

Ends the week having done more than usual and produced less than usual. Adds two locations to next week's schedule. Starts Monday feeling behind before the week begins. The mission is somewhere in the background, but it hasn't been in the foreground in a while. The slow week feels personal.

Recruiter B

Ends the week with one sign-up that came from reconnecting to the reason she does this. Doesn't add new locations. Looks at next week's schedule and asks what she can do differently at two existing locations. The slow week was a signal, not an indictment. She knows why she's going back out Monday.

Your Turn

When your numbers aren't moving, what's your first instinct — and when did you last reconnect to why the program matters?

Be honest about both. Do you reach for more activity, or do you stop and ask what's different about how you're working? And when's the last time you thought about the actual reason the program exists — not the description, the reason?
