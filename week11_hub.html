Say Something:
How This Team Communicates

Good teams don't wait for the right moment to raise something. They say it when they notice it — even when it's small, even when they're not sure it's their place. The cost of silence is almost always higher than the cost of the conversation that didn't happen.

Why This Week Exists

The interactive piece for Week 11 was missed. No one flagged it for two weeks. That's not a failure of any one person — it's a communication pattern worth examining together. Leadership can miss things. So can teammates. The question this week isn't who dropped the ball. It's: when you notice something is off, do you say something? This week is about building the kind of team where the answer is always yes.

Direction 1
Raising Something Up

How to bring something to your manager's attention without it feeling like a complaint. The difference between "I noticed something" and "I have a problem with something."

Direction 2
Raising Something Lateral

Peer to peer. When something a teammate is doing affects your work or the team's output, saying it directly — not going around, not going silent.

Direction 3
Raising Something You Noticed

The hardest one. When something is missing or wrong and it's not technically your responsibility — but you noticed it. This is the one that changes team culture.
