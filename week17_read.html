The Two-Step
Reactivation

Email opens the door. A real conversation walks through it. Neither one alone is enough — but together they brought back four donors this week who were on their way to the archive list.

The Difference Between Lapsed and Lost

When a donor misses one event, they are lapsed. When they miss two or three and have not responded to outreach, they are drifting. When they have been called twice, emailed once, and still nothing — then they are probably lost and it is time to archive. But between lapsed and lost, there is a window. That window is what reactivation is about.

A lapsed donor is not someone who decided to leave. In most cases they are someone whose life got busy, whose schedule shifted, whose daycare situation changed, or who just got out of the habit. They may have every intention of coming back. They just have not been given a specific reason to do it right now. The reactivation call is that reason.

A Patricia Moment
Patricia emailed Teddy and Sully's mom again.

Teddy and Sully have not donated since July. Their mom has not responded to two emails. Patricia sent a third email this week. The subject line was "We Miss Teddy and Sully!" Patricia considered two exclamation points but decided that was too much. Patricia is waiting to hear back. Patricia is also preparing a fourth email for next week just in case.

The problem is not the subject line. Teddy and Sully's mom is not going to respond to a fourth email when she did not respond to three. She needs a phone call and a specific offer. Patricia does not know this because Patricia has not tried anything other than email.

The Two-Step Sequence
1
Email to reopen the door — warm, not administrative
The reactivation email is not a reminder about dates. It is a reconnection that acknowledges the absence and frames the return in terms of impact. "We have missed seeing [dog's name] at our events. We understand schedules change, but the impact [dog's name] makes in saving lives is real, and we would love to figure out a way to make it work again." Then offer specific options: their current location, the office, a different daycare that might work better. Make the door easy to walk through.
2
Phone call or in-person follow-up to close
After the email goes out, the conversation that brings them back almost never happens over email. It happens on a phone call or in person at a recruiting event when you see them. That is where you find out what actually changed — the schedule, the daycare, the life situation. And it is where you can offer a solution they did not know was possible: a different location, a different day, an office appointment, a flexible timing option. The email opens the conversation. You close it.

An email tells them you noticed they were gone. A conversation finds out why — and gives you something to actually fix.

What the Email Needs to Say
The Reconnection Email — Core Elements
Open with:
The dog's name and how much you have missed seeing them. Personal, not administrative. This is not a system notification — it is a message from a person who actually knows the dog.
Acknowledge:
That schedules change and life happens. Do not make them feel guilty for being gone. Make them feel welcome to come back.
Frame the impact:
"The impact that [dog's name] makes in donations is real, and we would love to reconnect and see if we can figure out a schedule that works." This is why the return matters — not to your numbers, but to the dogs who need blood.
Offer options:
Their current location at a specific date. The office. Another nearby daycare. Give them two or three concrete options so the path back is clear, not vague.
Make it easy:
Your phone number. A direct reply. No forms, no portals, no "check the website." Tell them to respond to you specifically and you will handle the rest.
Three Types of Lapsed Donors
The Schedule Shift
Life changed, not intent
Work schedule changed, kids' activities started, daycare day no longer works. The owner still wants to participate. They just need a new plan. Offer the alternative location or office appointment and they usually come back.
The Drifted Owner
Lost the habit
No specific reason, just fell off. The email reconnects the feeling. The in-person or phone conversation re-establishes the habit. These are the most responsive to the impact frame because guilt is not useful but purpose is.
The Daycare Dropout
No longer attending
The dog stopped going to the daycare where they were donating. Check whether they are at a different daycare that you also visit. If yes, offer to transfer them. If no, the office appointment is the option. This is worth solving because the dog is already screened.
