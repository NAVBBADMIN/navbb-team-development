Case Study · Week 16

Same Pipeline. Very Different Doors.

Scene 1 of 6
The Setup

Same sign-up count. Same territory size. One has a 36% show rate. The other has over 100%.

Recruiter A and Recruiter B both had solid months of sign-ups. Both are hitting their numbers at the recruitment stage. The collection team, however, sees something completely different when it shows up to each of their locations. One recruiter consistently has dogs walking through the door. The other consistently has empty spots on the board.

The difference is not in how they recruit. It is in what happens in the two weeks between sign-up and show-up.

Recruiter A: The Email Approach

Recruiter A follows preferred communication method. Three donors said email. She emails.

Recruiter A keeps meticulous records of preferred communication method. When a donor says email, she emails. The email goes out the week before the event. It has the date, the location, what to expect, and a reminder about hydration.

The email is informative and professionally written. Two of the three donors open it. One of those two is planning to come. The other saw it at 6am and forgot about it by noon. Recruiter A does not know any of this. She sent the email. In her mind, she has followed up.

Recruiter B: The Call Approach

Recruiter B calls at midday. And again at 2:30 if they do not pick up.

Recruiter B also has donors who said email as their preferred method. She emails them the quarterly dates when they sign up. After that, she calls. Two weeks out, midday. If they do not pick up, she leaves a voicemail and calls again at 2:30.

On the call, she uses the dog's name. She says she is looking forward to seeing them. She asks directly whether they are still planning to come. She reads their answer. A confident "yes" and a hesitant "yeah I think so" are different information that an email open rate will never give her. She knows before event day who is coming and who is not.

What the Voicemail Does

A voicemail from a real person sits differently than a reminder in a folder.

One of Recruiter B's donors is the type who never picks up the phone. He does not call back either. But when Recruiter B leaves a message using his dog Murphy's name and says she is personally hoping to see Murphy on Tuesday, something different happens in his brain than when he receives an email reminder.

He mentions it to his wife that evening. "Someone called about Murphy's blood donation thing." His wife says "oh you should really do that, Murphy loves those people." Murphy shows up on Tuesday.

Recruiter A sent the same donor an email. He did not open it. He did not bring Murphy. Murphy's spot on the board was empty. Recruiter A added him to her follow-up list for next month.

The Archive Decision

Recruiter B archives faster. And her pipeline is actually cleaner because of it.

Recruiter A

Has 28 pending donors. Some registered in May. She has not called any of them. She emails when the event approaches and notes whether they showed. Several have not shown in three months. They are still on the list. They make her show rate look worse. She keeps them because they might still come. They will not come.

Recruiter B

Called every pending donor within two weeks of sign-up. Called them again before the first event. Anyone who did not respond to two calls and an email got archived. Her pipeline is smaller. Every name on her list is someone she has spoken to who has confirmed they are still in. Her show rate reflects real donors, not stale names.

A 36% show rate on 28 pending donors is 10 actual donors. A 100% show rate on 12 real donors is 12 actual donors. Recruiter B has fewer names and more donations. The list is not the goal. The door is the goal.

Your Turn

Look at your pending donor list. When did you last actually speak to each of them? Not email — speak?

For anyone you have not spoken to since the sign-up conversation, that is your list for this week. Not to email. To call. What would you expect to find out if you called them right now?
