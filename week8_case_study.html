Four Maybes, Four Outcomes

~8 min · Click through each scene · One question at the end.
Scene 1 of 6
The Same Tuesday

Four maybes. Two recruiters. The same lobby, the same afternoon.

Recruiter B and Patricia are both at a location they've visited before. Each of them encounters four dog owners who say some version of "I'll think about it." Same people. Same situation. Different results by the end of the week.

The four maybes are: a dad who needs to ask his wife, a woman who's genuinely interested but worried about her dog's temperament, a man who's been coming to the daycare for two years and keeps saying next time, and a newer member who just signed up two weeks ago and says she's heard about it but wants to know more.

Watch how each recruiter handles each one.

Maybe #1 — The Spouse Defer

"Sounds great — I'll talk to my wife about it."

⚡ The Bypass

Dad is friendly, clearly interested, asks good questions. But he's not the decision-maker. When he says he'll talk to his wife, the standard response is to hand over a flyer and hope the conversation happens.

Patricia

Patricia gives dad the flyer. Patricia tells him the website is on the back. Patricia adds him to her email list. Dad puts the flyer in the glovebox. His wife never sees it. Patricia emails him in two weeks. He doesn't remember the conversation.

Recruiter B

She asks: "Would you mind if I emailed her directly with the information? That way she has it straight from me and can ask me questions if she has any." Dad says yes. She gets the email address from the daycare account. Mom emails her back that evening. Rocky is registered by Thursday.

Maybe #2 — The Temperament Worry

"I'd love to, but Daisy can be kind of nervous. I'm not sure she'd be a good fit."

⚡ The Cheerleader

This owner is interested but has a specific concern that no amount of program information will fully address. She needs to hear from someone who had the same concern and found out it was fine. That's not Recruiter B. That's another owner in the same lobby.

Patricia

Patricia explains that nervous dogs can still participate. Patricia describes the happy visit process. Patricia gives her a flyer. The owner nods and takes the flyer. She has the same concern she started with, plus a flyer.

Recruiter B

She sees an existing donor owner at the front desk. She says: "Hey, do you mind if I introduce you to someone for one second?" She makes the introduction. The donor owner says her dog was nervous at first too and now loves coming. The conversation lasts four minutes. Daisy's owner signs up.

Maybe #3 — The Serial Next-Timer

"I keep meaning to do it. Maybe next time."

⚡ The Breadcrumb

This owner has heard the pitch. He knows what the program is. He's been saying next time for eight months. The problem isn't information. The problem is he needs a nudge that doesn't come from the recruiter.

Patricia

Patricia gives him the same pitch again. She mentions the website. She adds him to the email list. He has received four emails from Patricia. He opens them and then doesn't click anything. He will say "next time" again next month.

Recruiter B

She stops pitching and starts planting. She mentions to the staff member at the front desk that this guy's been thinking about it for a while — does she know him well? Staff member says yes, he's been coming for two years. She asks if she'd mind mentioning it next time she sees him. Staff member texts her the following Thursday to say he signed up.

Maybe #4 — The Newest Member

"I just heard about this a couple weeks ago. What's it actually like?"

⚡ The Cheerleader

This owner joined the daycare recently and is genuinely curious, not resistant. She wants real answers from real experience. This is the easiest Murphy deployment in the book.

Patricia

Patricia explains the program thoroughly. Patricia answers all her questions accurately. Patricia hands her a flyer. The owner says "that actually sounds really great" and leaves. Patricia marks her as a warm lead. She does not sign up that day.

Recruiter B

She says: "Honestly, you should hear it from someone who's been through it." She introduces her to an existing donor owner who's been in the program for a year. That owner answers the "what's it actually like" question in a way no pitch can. She signs up before she leaves the lobby.
