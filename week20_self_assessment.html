A Patricia Moment
Patricia scored herself a 5 on Question 1.

Patricia explained that she registers every maybe so that she never loses a lead, and that this is the most responsible thing a recruiter can do. She has 47 pending donors. She has contacted none of them since registering. Patricia also scored herself a 5 on the pending list question because she knows exactly where the list is.

Question 1 of 6 - Sign-Up Integrity
When a parent is not sure yet, I take their phone number and call them back. I do not register the donor in FETCH as a placeholder.
The registration should mean the parent said yes. Contact information is how you hold a maybe.
Question 2 of 6 - Sign-Up Integrity
Before a parent signs up, I say the monthly commitment out loud, name the day, and give them an easy way to say no.
For example: this is once a month, we are here every four weeks on a Monday, and if it cannot work, just tell me.
Question 3 of 6 - Daycare Sign-Ups
When a daycare signs a parent up without me, I call that parent myself and confirm the commitment.
A sign-up you did not take is a sign-up you have not confirmed.
Question 4 of 6 - Pending List
I review my pending list regularly and contact anything that has sat for months with no communication.
Name the location and date. Ask if it works. Say you are happy to remove them if not.
Question 5 of 6 - Decisions
When a donor is not going to work out, I archive them and write the reason in the notes. I do not leave them to sit.
Happy visits are two to three tries, and only work when they are consistent. Past that, it needs a decision.
Question 6 of 6 - Verify First
When I hear a medical claim from an owner or daycare, I ask for vet records and pass them to the medical team. I do not repeat the diagnosis word.
Heartworm versus hookworm. Pneumonia versus kennel cough. The label matters, and units can be pulled when it is wrong.
