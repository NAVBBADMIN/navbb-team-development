This Week
Write down exactly what you do for each of the three situations.

For medical questions, aggression flags, and compassion calls, document your specific response: what you say to the owner, who you contact, what you do while you wait. Then identify one situation from the past month where you handled it yourself when you should have stepped back. Bring both to the meeting.

A Patricia Moment
Patricia is mapping her handoff points right now.

For medical questions, Patricia has written: "Look it up online, share the main points, then suggest they call their vet." Patricia does not see the issue with this. Patricia has also written that for aggression flags she will "note it in the system and let the collection team make the call." Patricia considers herself thorough. Patricia has not yet completed the compassion calls section because she has not made a compassion call before. Patricia is considering sending an email instead.

Situation 1: A Medical Question Comes Up

What exactly do you say to the owner or daycare manager in the moment to hold the conversation without answering the clinical question? Then who do you contact and how fast?

Who do you contact and how?

Situation 2: A Dog Shows an Aggression Signal

What specifically counts as a signal for you? And what do you say to the owner to decline the sign-up in a way that is honest, kind, and does not damage the facility relationship?

What do you say to the owner?

Situation 3: A Dog Receives a Permanent Retirement

What does your outreach look like? Do you call or email? What do you say? Do you mention the free-blood-for-life benefit and if so, when and how?

One Situation You Handled Yourself When You Should Have Stepped Back

Honest look at the past month. A medical question you answered. An aggression flag you let slide. A difficult parent conversation you handled alone. What happened and what would you do differently?
