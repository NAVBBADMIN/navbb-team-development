The Situation

Three donors who had gone quiet. One recruiter, one week, three reactivations.

Murphy Baker had signed up at Harmony but her work schedule changed and the donation day no longer worked. Andy Pants had drifted and missed multiple events with no explanation. Brody had been retired from unresponsiveness after his original daycare stopped working out.

All three were candidates for the archive list. Instead, all three came back. The approach was the same in each case: a warm reconnection email to open the door, followed by a real conversation to walk through it. The specifics of what brought each one back were different. The sequence was identical.

Murphy Baker — The Schedule Problem

The daycare day stopped working. A different location was the solution.

Reactivated and screened Monday

Murphy Baker originally signed up at Harmony. The recruiter had been in back-and-forth contact with her for a while trying to find a way to make the schedule work. The Harmony donation day had shifted and Murphy's work schedule did not line up anymore.

The email acknowledged that the original schedule was not working and offered an alternative: Dog Day Afternoon on a Monday. The follow-up conversation confirmed the new schedule worked and locked in a specific screening date.

What made it work

The recruiter stayed in the problem instead of giving up on it. When the original location stopped working, she offered an alternative rather than waiting for Murphy's mom to figure it out herself. The dog was already screened and committed. The only barrier was logistics. Solve the logistics and the donor comes back.

Andy Pants — The Drifted Donor

No specific reason. Multiple forms of contact. The impact frame brought him back.

Back in rotation and donating

Andy Pants had no obvious reason for drifting. He had just stopped showing up. The recruiter sent an email, called, and ran into his owner at a recruiting event. Each touchpoint used the impact frame: the donation Andy Pants makes is real, it saves lives, we miss seeing him.

The recruiting event was where the conversation closed. Seeing the recruiter in person, at the daycare where Andy Pants attends, with a specific next date on the calendar, made it concrete in a way that email alone could not.

What made it work

All forms of communication, in sequence. Email to start the conversation. Phone to follow up. In-person to close it. Donors who drifted without a specific reason often respond to the combination of impact framing and convenience — when the recruiter is right there and the next date is close, returning is easier than not returning.

Brody — The Location Change

The original daycare stopped working. He was already at a new one we visit.

Back at Bark and Boarding

Brody had been retired from unresponsiveness after his original daycare situation changed. The recruiter sent a reconnection email. On the follow-up conversation, she found out that Brody had moved to Bark and Boarding — a location the team already visits.

This was the easiest kind of reactivation: the dog was already screened, already committed, and already attending a location the team goes to. The only barrier was that nobody had connected those two facts. The email opened the door. The conversation revealed the path.

What made it work

A reactivation email often surfaces information you would not have otherwise gotten. The owner knew Brody had moved daycares. She had not thought to tell the program because she did not know the program visited that location too. The conversation is where you find out what changed — and sometimes what changed is actually good news.

What All Three Had in Common

The email was personal. The follow-up found the actual reason. The offer was specific.

In all three cases, the reactivation email was not a reminder or a notification. It acknowledged the dog by name, acknowledged the absence without guilt-tripping, and framed the return in terms of impact. "We understand that schedules change, but the impact that [dog] makes is real and we would love to reconnect."

In all three cases, the conversation that actually brought the donor back was not over email. It was by phone or in person. The email got the owner thinking about it. The conversation found the specific barrier and offered the specific solution.

And in all three cases, the recruiter did not wait for the owner to figure out how to come back on their own. She gave them a concrete path: a specific date, a specific location, a specific next step. That is what turned a maybe into a done.
