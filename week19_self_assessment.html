A Patricia Moment
Patricia answered a medical question this week.

A daycare manager asked Patricia about kennel cough transmission. Patricia Googled it. Patricia shared three bullet points from a 2019 article in the team chat and also forwarded them to the manager. Patricia scored herself a 4 on this self-assessment because she took the question seriously and followed up promptly. Dr. Blake spent 45 minutes recalibrating the conversation with the manager that afternoon. Patricia considers her research skills an asset.

Question 1 of 6 - Medical Questions
When a daycare owner or parent asks me a medical question about disease transmission, kennel cough, illness in the facility, or anything clinical, my first move is to route it to Dr. Blake or Dr. Galanis — not to answer it myself.
Not after I have looked it up. Not after I have tried to help. First move.
Question 2 of 6 - Medical Questions
I know the difference between sharing a fact and giving a medical opinion — and I know which one is appropriate for a recruiter.
A fact: "Our veterinary team takes infectious disease very seriously." An opinion: "Kennel cough has an incubation period of 3-10 days, so your dogs probably did not get it from our visit." One is yours to say. One is not.
Question 3 of 6 - Aggression
If a dog snaps at me during a meet-and-greet, or a daycare mentions bite history, or an owner uses the word unpredictable, I do not sign that dog up — regardless of how the rest of the conversation goes.
Not "I will note it and let the collection team assess." Not "I will flag it but sign them up anyway." The answer is no sign-up.
Question 4 of 6 - Aggression
I know what to say to an owner when I need to decline their dog for aggression concerns — in a way that is honest, kind, and does not damage the relationship with the facility.
The owner who just heard their dog is not a good fit is still a person at a facility you will return to. The conversation matters.
Question 5 of 6 - Compassion Calls
When a dog receives a permanent medical retirement, I make a personal phone call to the owner — not just a notification or an email.
A phone call to check in, to thank them, to acknowledge what the dog contributed, and to make sure they know about the free-blood-for-life benefit.
Question 6 of 6 - Compassion Calls
I have told owners about the free-blood-for-life benefit that every blood donor dog is entitled to.
Not as a pitch. As something real that exists and that they should know about — especially when a dog is facing a health challenge.
