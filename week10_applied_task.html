This Week — Two Tasks
Write the sentence. Then go where the email hasn't worked.

Task one: Write one sentence that communicates the urgency of the program in human terms. Not what the program offers — what happens when there isn't enough. Then use it in a real conversation this week and report what happened. Task two: Name one location you've been trying to reach by email or phone without success. Plan an unannounced visit this week and document what you find when you show up.

A Patricia Moment
Patricia wrote her urgency sentence.

Patricia's sentence is: "It's a great program and the dogs really enjoy the happy visits!" Patricia considers this urgent. Patricia has emailed four locations without a response for three months. Patricia has not visited any of them. Patricia is waiting to hear back. Patricia has been waiting since April.

Task 1 — Write Your Urgency Sentence

One sentence. Not what the program is — what happens when there isn't enough blood available for a dog in an emergency. Make it human. Make it specific. Say it out loud before you write it down.

Examples to inspire — not to copy
"When a dog comes into an emergency needing a transfusion and we don't have enough blood available, that dog doesn't make it home."
"Every donor dog like yours is the reason another family gets to bring their dog home from the hospital."
"There are dogs in surgeries right now whose outcome depends on whether dogs like yours are in the program."

After you've used it — what happened?

Describe the conversation. Did the person's energy shift when you said it? Did it lead to a different kind of question than you usually get? Did it close? Be specific about what changed and what didn't.
