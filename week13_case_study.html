Three Months Later

~8 min · Click through each scene · One question at the end.
Scene 1 of 6
The Setup

Two recruiters. Same location. Same slow month. Very different reads on what it means.

Recruiter A and Recruiter B both had a stretch where conversations were happening but the board wasn't moving. Plenty of interested parents. Almost no sign-ups. Both are trying to figure out what to do with that.

Recruiter A concludes the approach isn't working. Recruiter B concludes the pipeline is filling. Watch what happens over the next three months.

Recruiter A — The Pivot

Recruiter A decides the conversations aren't converting. She changes her approach.

After three slow weeks, Recruiter A decides the problem is the pitch. She rewrites her opening. She starts leading with statistics instead of stories. She adds two new locations to get more at-bats. She stops following up on conversations from more than two weeks ago and focuses on new contacts instead.

Her logic is reasonable: if the conversations were working, they'd be converting. Since they're not converting, something about the conversations must be wrong. Change the approach. Move on. Get fresh contacts.

Recruiter B — The Hold

Recruiter B decides the slow weeks are data, not a verdict.

Recruiter B has a different read. She had good conversations. People seemed genuinely interested. The fact that they haven't signed up yet doesn't mean they won't. It means they haven't yet. There's a difference.

She keeps going to the same locations. She has good conversations with new people and occasionally reconnects with someone she talked to before. She doesn't chase anyone who hasn't responded, but she doesn't write them off either. She keeps the door open by keeping showing up.

Month Two — The Trigger

A parent who talked to Recruiter B six weeks ago signs up. She has no idea what triggered it.

The parent had a casual conversation with Recruiter B at the daycare in early October. She said she was definitely interested. She left without signing up. Recruiter B saw her again two weeks later and they said hi but didn't have a full conversation. Then nothing for a month.

On a Tuesday in November, the parent's dog had a vet visit. The vet mentioned blood donors during the checkup. The parent went home, found the website, and registered. She had remembered the recruiter's name. She mentioned it in the registration notes.

Recruiter B didn't do anything in month two to cause this sign-up. The original conversation did the work. The recruiter just hadn't declared the person lost before the trigger arrived.

Month Three — The Count

Three months later. Same slow stretch at the start. Very different pipelines.

Recruiter A

Changed her pitch, added locations, moved on from old contacts. Got some new sign-ups from the new approach. Also lost a handful of delayed conversions that never had a chance to come back because she'd moved on. Her pipeline is wider. It's not deeper. She doesn't have data on what made her best conversations stick because she didn't stay with them long enough to find out.

Recruiter B

Kept her approach, kept her locations, kept the door open. Got the same new sign-ups she would have gotten anyway — and got three delayed conversions from conversations she'd had in months one and two. She now knows which parts of her pitch seem to produce delayed sign-ups because she was there when they came back. She's better at the conversation than she was three months ago.

Your Turn

Think of a conversation you had in the last 60–90 days that felt promising. Is that person still in your pipeline, or did you move on?

Not a follow-up question — a real one. If you moved on, what made you decide the conversation wasn't going to convert? And if you could go back, what would you do differently?
