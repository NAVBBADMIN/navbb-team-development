The Setup

Three donors who were no longer active. Three different situations. Three recruiters who stayed in the relationship.

Mo was medically retired because of allergy medication. Ripley was retired in September for medication. The woman at Pet Suites Grove City signed up months ago and never heard anything. None of them were in active donor status this week. All three of them came back.

In each case, the recruiter had not walked away from the parent. That ongoing presence is what made the return possible. These are not comeback stories that started last week. They are the result of relationships that kept going even after the dog stopped donating.

Mo — The Communicative Parent

Mo was retired. His parents kept talking. Hannah kept tracking.

Reactivated and confirmed for November

Mo has been a donor since 2024. When his allergy medication came up in his system, he was correctly retired. His parents did not disappear. They kept checking in with Hannah at the daycare visits, updating her on how Mo was doing, telling her when the medication was expected to clear up.

Hannah kept notes. When the medication cleared, she flagged Mo for reactivation, ran it through the medical team, and got him back into the system. Mom and dad already know about the November date because they have been in communication the whole time. The reactivation happened because the relationship never stopped.

What made it work

Hannah did not need to chase Mo back. The parents drove the communication because they wanted their dog to donate again. The recruiter's job was to receive that communication, track it, and act on it at the right moment. Parents who stay engaged during a retirement are the easiest reactivations. Keep the relationship warm and they will tell you when the time is right.

Ripley — The Parent Who Asked

Ripley was retired in September. Her mom asked how to get her back at the very next visit.

Back and donating

Ripley was medically retired in September because of medication. Donna was at Holiday Barn Glen Allen that same month. Ripley's mom approached her and asked the exact question a recruiter hopes to hear: "What do we need to do to get her back into donations?"

Donna took that question directly to Dr. Galanis at the visit. She found out what the timeline was and what the parent needed to do. Mom stopped the medication and provided the documentation. When Donna was back the following month, all the pieces were in place. Ripley was reactivated. The whole thing happened because Donna was still there, the parent was still engaged, and the path was made clear the moment the question was asked.

What made it work

Donna did not make a medical decision. She bridged the parent to the person who could. She asked Dr. Galanis the right question: what does this parent need to do? Then she took the answer back to the parent. When a parent asks how to get their dog back, the answer is not a guess — it is a process, and the recruiter's job is to connect them to it.

Grove City — The Forgotten Sign-Up

She signed up months ago. No one called. She had a disappointed attitude. Tracy gave her news.

Re-engaged on the spot

Tracy was at Pet Suites Grove City during a screening day. She approached a woman about the program. The woman said she had already signed up a while back. She had not heard anything since. Her tone carried some disappointment — she had said yes to something that seemed to have gone nowhere.

Tracy said: "Great news. We are starting in two weeks." The woman's whole demeanor changed. She got excited. She had not given up. She had just lost the thread. One sentence reconnected her to something she had already agreed to do. That is not a medical reactivation. That is what happens when a sign-up goes stale and someone shows up at the right time with the right information.

What made it work

Tracy did not over-engineer it. She gave the parent a specific, close date and a concrete piece of news. Stale sign-ups are not lost donors. They are parents who said yes once and never heard a second thing. A specific date is all most of them need to feel like they are back in the process.

What All Three Had in Common

The recruiter did not disappear when the donation stopped.

Mo's parents stayed in contact because Hannah was still showing up at their daycare. Ripley's mom asked how to come back because Donna was standing right there when the thought crossed her mind. The Grove City parent re-engaged because Tracy approached her as if she mattered, even though her name had been sitting in a list going nowhere.

In all three cases, the return was possible because the recruiter had not moved on when the dog stopped donating. The relationship kept going. And when the situation changed — when the medication cleared, when the parent asked a question, when the program finally got started — someone was still there to catch it.

The donors who come back are the ones whose recruiters stayed in the relationship. The ones who get permanently lost are the ones whose recruiters moved on the moment the dog left active status.
