Question 1 of 6 · Trust-Building
When someone says "I'll think about it," my first instinct is to try something beyond giving them a flyer or adding them to a follow-up list.
Not a different version of the same pitch — something that addresses the trust gap specifically.
Question 2 of 6 · Trust-Building
I can tell the difference between someone who needs more information and someone who needs peer confirmation before they'll commit.
These look the same on the surface. They are not the same thing, and they need different responses.
Question 3 of 6 · Decision-Maker Identification
When the person in front of me says they need to talk to their spouse, I try to reach the decision-maker directly rather than waiting for the message to be passed along.
Asking permission to email, calling on the spot, getting contact information — anything that removes the middleman.
Question 4 of 6 · Decision-Maker Identification
Before I finish a conversation with a "maybe," I know whether the person I'm talking to is actually the one who will make the decision.
Sometimes the person picking up is fully in charge. Sometimes they're not. Knowing which is which changes what you do next.
Question 5 of 6 · Cheerleader Use
I know who the Murphy is at each of my locations — an existing donor owner who, if asked, would talk to a hesitant parent.
Not just that they're a long-term donor — that they're the kind of person who would be happy to say "here's what it's actually like" to a stranger in the lobby.
Question 6 of 6 · Cheerleader Use
I've made a direct introduction between a hesitant parent and an existing donor owner — and it moved the needle.
Not just mentioning that other dogs do it — actually facilitating a real conversation between a maybe and a Murphy.
