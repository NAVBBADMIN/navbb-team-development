A Patricia Moment
Patricia has sent Teddy and Sully's mom three emails.

Teddy and Sully have been absent since July. Patricia emailed in August. Patricia emailed again in September with a better subject line. Patricia emailed again this week with an exclamation point. Patricia has not called. Patricia has not offered an alternative location. Patricia has not asked what changed. Patricia is considering a fourth email with a different font. Patricia scored herself a 4 on this self-assessment because she follows up consistently.

Question 1 of 6 - The First Response
When a donor misses their second consecutive event without explanation, I reach out with something personal - not a system notification or a generic reminder.
An email or message that uses the dog's name, acknowledges the absence, and opens the door to a real conversation about what changed.
Question 2 of 6 - The Follow-Up
After the reconnection email, I follow up by phone or in person - not with another email.
The email opens the door. The conversation walks through it. A second email just opens a slightly wider door that nobody is still walking through.
Question 3 of 6 - Finding the Reason
When I talk to a lapsed donor, my goal is to find out what specifically changed - not to remind them of dates or confirm they are still interested in general.
Schedule changed? Daycare changed? Life got busy? The specific reason determines the specific solution. Without the reason, all you can offer is a date.
Question 4 of 6 - Offering Alternatives
When a lapsed donor's situation has changed, I offer specific alternatives - a different location, an office appointment, a different day - rather than just hoping they figure out how to come back.
A screened dog who can no longer attend their original daycare is not necessarily lost. They might go to a different daycare we visit, or be able to do an office appointment. That is worth exploring.
Question 5 of 6 - The Impact Frame
In reactivation outreach, I lead with the impact the donor makes - not the administrative fact that they have missed events.
"We have missed seeing Murphy" lands differently than "Murphy has missed two donation events." One is about the relationship and the impact. The other is a status update.
Question 6 of 6 - Knowing When to Stop
I have a clear threshold for when a reactivation attempt has run its course and the donor should be archived — and I apply it consistently rather than continuing to try indefinitely.
Reactivation is worth a real attempt. But there is a point where continued effort takes energy away from donors who will actually show up. Knowing when to stop is part of the skill.
