This Week — Two Things
Your sentence. Your one reactive decision.

Write one sentence that explains why you do this job — not the description, the actual reason it matters to you. Keep it. Use it when things get hard. Then identify one decision you made in the last few weeks that was reactive rather than strategic — and write what the mission-grounded version of that decision would have looked like. Both go to the meeting.

A Patricia Moment
Patricia wrote her sentence.

Patricia's sentence is: "I recruit donors for a canine blood bank because this is an important program that saves dogs' lives." Patricia considers this her sentence. Patricia has not thought about why the program matters in six weeks. Patricia's sentence is technically accurate and emotionally inert. Patricia will share it at the next meeting. Patricia is proud of it.

Part 1 — Your One Sentence

Not the program description. Not "it saves lives" as a phrase. The actual reason it matters to you — what you think about when the work is hard and you're deciding whether it's worth it. Say it out loud before you write it. Write the version that would make someone feel something.

Where did you use it this week?

Did you say it in a conversation, or something close to it? What happened? If you didn't use it yet, write your plan for when and how you will.

Part 2 — One Reactive Decision

A schedule change you agreed to because a partner asked. A location you added because your numbers were low. A follow-up you sent because it felt like doing something. Name the decision and what made it reactive rather than strategic.

What would the strategic version look like?

If you had run the decision through a mission filter first — does this serve the program's ability to collect blood consistently — what would you have decided? What would have been different?
