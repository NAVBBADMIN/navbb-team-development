A Patricia Moment
Patricia scored herself a 4 on upward communication.

Patricia believes she communicates well with leadership. Patricia sends thorough, well-framed emails. Patricia sent one such email last month about something she noticed. The email was four paragraphs. The key point was in paragraph three. Patricia has not yet confirmed whether the email was read. Patricia considers this a strong communication record.

Question 1 of 6 · Upward Communication
When I notice something is off — a gap, an error, something missing — I flag it to leadership the same day, even if it might already be handled.
Not after confirming it's a real issue, not after seeing if anyone else raises it — same day, quick flag.
Question 2 of 6 · Upward Communication
When I bring something to my manager's attention, I keep it simple — one or two sentences — rather than building a full case first.
The instinct to over-explain or perfectly frame a concern before raising it often delays the message and buries the point.
Question 3 of 6 · Lateral Communication
When something a peer is doing — or not doing — affects my work, I say something to them directly rather than working around it or going to a manager first.
The direct conversation, not the workaround or the escalation.
Question 4 of 6 · Lateral Communication
When a peer's work is affecting the team's output — not just mine — I say something, even if it means a mildly uncomfortable conversation.
Not just when it directly impacts you — when you can see it affecting the team.
Question 5 of 6 · Proactive Flagging
When I notice something that isn't my direct responsibility — something missing, something wrong, something unclear — I flag it rather than assuming someone else will.
The thing that isn't technically yours to notice but that you noticed anyway.
Question 6 of 6 · Proactive Flagging
I check in proactively with leadership and peers — not just when something's wrong, but to keep communication channels warm and normal.
The Chippendale principle. Consistent presence communicates investment. It also makes raising a real issue feel natural when one comes up.
